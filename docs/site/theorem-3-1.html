Theorem 3.1

The closed-form rotating solution at the heart of the document — and exactly what the verification ladder certifies about it.

Statement

For the vortex model with the Chaplygin constant C_Ch, the document constructs (Section 5) the following solution, a Lagrange-type relative equilibrium with radial modulation:

r_k(t) = √C_Ch · (1 + ε·cos(ωt + 2πk/3)),   k = 0, 1, 2
θ_k(t) = ω·t + 2π·k/3
with the frequency and amplitude fixed by the Chaplygin constant:
ω = (2π/T)·e^(C_Ch/π),    ε = 1/(e^(C_Ch/π) − 1)

Two structural facts follow immediately from the form itself and are the subject of ladder check V1:

  • Equilateral choreography. The angular positions differ by exactly 2π/3 at every instant — the three vortices form an equilateral triangle that rotates rigidly, the vortex analogue of the classical Lagrange solution of the three-body problem.
  • Periodicity. The radial modulation repeats with period T_r = 2π/ω; the closed form is a periodic orbit of the model.

What the ladder certifies — and what it only records

An honest boundary separates two kinds of statements about the closed form:

Certified (pass/fail)

The 2π/3 choreography and the periodicity are properties of the formulas themselves: substituting the closed form, the angular separation is 2π/3 up to float64 argument reduction, and r_k(t + T_r) = r_k(t) up to the same round-off. These hold for every C_Ch > 0.01, on every probed time window (V1), and the same quantities are pinned by independent pytest reference values.

Recorded (diagnostic)

The combination C_Ch(t) = r²(θ̇ − q·A_θ) with A_θ = 1/r oscillates together with the radial modulation — by construction of the model, not by a failure of conservation. Section 6 of the document records its endpoint drift over [0, 100·T] as a diagnostic; the window-independent conserved quantities of the dynamics are the vortex integrals H, P, Q, I, certified numerically in V3–V4.

The numerical twin: the Lagrange triangle

Theorem 3.1 has an exact numerical counterpart that needs no closed form at all. Take three equal point vortices on an equilateral triangle of side a and integrate the Kirchhoff equations with RK4. Classical theory says the triangle rotates rigidly with angular velocity

ω_L = 3Γ / (2π·a²)

— for Γ = 1, a = 1 that is ω_L = 3/(2π) ≈ 0.4775. Ladder check V2 confirms both halves of this statement numerically: the side lengths stay equilateral to ≤ 1e-10 over multiple full rotations, and the measured rotation frequency matches 3Γ/(2πa²) to a relative error ≤ 1e-6. Checks V3–V4 then verify the conservation of the four classical integrals along the same trajectory — first for the symmetric configuration, then for unequal circulations Γ = (1, 2, 3) from a generic triangle, where no symmetry can carry the answer.

Why «Lagrange»

In the gravitational three-body problem, the equilateral configuration rotating rigidly is one of Euler–Lagrange's central configurations (1772, the essai sur le problème des trois corps); in the vortex world the same triangle is an exact relative equilibrium known since Kirchhoff. Theorem 3.1 is the TRIVORTEX document's version of this classical object, dressed in the Chaplygin gauge: the radial modulation ε·cos(...) is the model-specific addition that the topological integral controls. The resonance between the gravitational and vortex stories is the quiet engine of the whole document — the reason a "three-body problem" paper is allowed to compute Berry phases and Chern numbers on the side.

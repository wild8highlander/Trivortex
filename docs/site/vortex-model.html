The Vortex Model

How the three-body problem becomes a problem about vortices, flux and a topological integral.

From bodies to vortices

The classical three-body problem asks for the motion of three masses under mutual Newtonian attraction; its vortex twin replaces masses by vortices with circulations and topological charges q_k = (1, −1, 1). The trade is deliberate: point-vortex dynamics are Hamiltonian and first-order in time, their configuration space is the plane, and their conserved quantities are algebraic rather than transcendental. What is lost in literal gravitational realism is gained in exact statements — the Lagrange equilateral rotation becomes a theorem one can pin to machine precision, and the chaotic N-vortex regime remains available for study as N grows.

TRIVORTEX goes one step further than plain point vortices: each vortex sits in an effective magnetic flux, so the model lives at the intersection of hydrodynamics and the Aharonov–Bohm physics of charged particles on a lattice. The single-vortex conserved combination is the Chaplygin topological integral C_Ch = r² · (θ̇ − q·A_θ),   A_θ = 1/r which binds the angular momentum to the gauge flux — the quantity Section 6 of the core document records along trajectories.

The Hamiltonian

Section 4 of the document builds an L×L Hofstadter Hamiltonian with N_v embedded vortices: a square lattice with periodic boundary conditions, Peierls phases φ = 2πα(ix−1)dy from the uniform flux α plus vortex-induced AB phases, and a diagonal potential that falls off as the inverse square of the distance to each vortex core, weighted by its charge. The result is a Hermitian matrix whose spectrum is analysed by the spectral-statistics machinery of Section 7 (GUE/GOE/Poisson classification, ⟨r⟩ statistic, KS tests against the classical random-matrix ensembles).

Spectral statistics

Unfolded spacings, ⟨r⟩ statistic against GUE (0.5996), GOE (0.5307) and Poisson (0.3863) references, KS tests, number variance and spectral form factor (Sections 7, 13, 20–21).

Quantum-topological

Berry phase, Chern numbers via Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki, TKNN Hall conductance, Dirac-cone analysis (Sections 16–19) — the quantum layer of the same model.

Chaos diagnostics

Lyapunov exponents for vortex vs Newtonian dynamics, a permutation test with Z-scores, KAM-tori fraction and Poincaré sections (Section 22).

Real systems

Sun–Earth–Moon, α Centauri and Pluto–Charon–Nix presets map astronomical masses, distances and periods into the model (Section 10, dedicated page).

Where the model stands in the literature

The three-body problem needs no introduction — from Newton through Poincaré's memoir and the discovery of chaos to Chenciner–Montgomery's figure-eight choreography (2000) and the modern zoo of choreographic solutions. The point-vortex formulation goes back to Kirchhoff and to the exact results of the 19th century (Thomson's vortex atoms, the Lagrange triangle of vortices); Chaplygin's name is attached to the invariants of vortex systems and to the gas-dynamical reduction bearing his name. TRIVORTEX borrows the choreography viewpoint (exact relative equilibria) and the vortex formalism (Hamiltonian, invariants), then adds its own layer: the AB-flux gauge potential that turns the angular momentum into a topological integral. The document is honest about this lineage — it is a model study with exact checks, not a claim about planetary motion.
